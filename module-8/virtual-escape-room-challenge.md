
# *Virtual Escape Room Challenge*


## Purpose

Most professional work does not fail because people lack technical skill. It fails because a team could not coordinate, could not disagree productively, or could not decide.

This activity puts you in a situation where the only way forward is through other people. You will work with a group of classmates to solve a timed online Escape Room — a series of puzzles that no single person can reasonably complete alone within the time limit.

By the end, you will have practiced:

- **Teamwork** — working toward a shared goal under time pressure
- **Communication** — explaining your thinking clearly and listening to others
- **Negotiation** — resolving competing ideas about how to proceed
- **Problem-solving** — breaking complex challenges into workable parts
- **Compromise** — letting go of your preferred approach when the team needs you to

> 📌 **Important**
> Escaping the room is the *objective*. Demonstrating effective teamwork and communication is the *assignment*. A team that escapes but communicates poorly has not fully succeeded. A team that runs out of time but collaborates well has still accomplished something real.

## STEP 1: Connect With Your Team

You will be **randomly assigned to a team of approximately 10 students**. Team assignments will be posted in the Canvas.

Once your team is posted:

1. **Reach out within 48 hours.** Post an introduction in your team's group channel or discussion thread.
2. **Propose availability.** Share two or three time windows that work for you.
3. **Lock in a single session time** that works for as many members as possible.
4. **Confirm the platform** you will use to meet (video call, group chat, or whatever your team agrees on).

> ⚠️ **Plan for the full duration.** The Escape Room takes **180–200 minutes**. Schedule a block with buffer time. Splitting the session across multiple days is discouraged — momentum matters, and partial context makes the puzzles significantly harder.

> 💡 **If scheduling stalls,** do not wait silently. Someone has to move first. Propose a specific date and time, and ask people to confirm or counter. Taking initiative when a group is stuck is itself part of what this assignment is teaching.

---

## STEP 2: Establish Ground Rules Before You Start

Spend the **first 10–15 minutes** of your session agreeing on how you will work together. Teams that skip this step almost always lose time later to confusion and conflict.

Decide as a group:

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Who will keep track of time? | Prevents the team from sinking 40 minutes into one puzzle |
| Who will record clues and findings? | Prevents solving the same thing twice |
| How will we make decisions when we disagree? | Majority vote? Try both? Defer to whoever is most confident? |
| How do we signal that we're stuck? | Creates permission to ask for help early |
| How do we make sure everyone is heard? | Large groups silence quiet members by default |

Write these decisions down. You will reference them in your reflection.

## STEP 3: Complete the Escape Room

Access the Escape Room link posted in the Canvas and work through it as a team.

### What Effective Teams Do

Use this as a working checklist during the activity:

- ✅ **Communicate clearly and respectfully** — say what you see, not just what you conclude
- ✅ **Share information immediately** — a clue in one person's head is useless to the team
- ✅ **Listen to different perspectives** — the person who sees it differently is often the person who solves it
- ✅ **Negotiate when opinions conflict** — argue about the idea, not about who said it
- ✅ **Compromise when needed** — sometimes the second-best approach that everyone commits to beats the best approach nobody executes
- ✅ **Divide responsibilities** — with ten people, parallel work is your biggest advantage
- ✅ **Handle disagreement professionally** — disagreement is useful; hostility is not
- ✅ **Keep the team focused** — someone needs to pull the group back on task, and it can be you

### Document Your Session

Capture evidence of your team's work:
- A screenshot of your completion screen (or final progress, if you do not finish)
- A brief note of your team members' names and who attended

## STEP 4: Participate as a Full Team Member

**Every student is expected to contribute actively.**

A ten-person team makes it easy to disappear. Resist that. Do not rely on one or two members to carry the challenge while the rest observe.

Active participation means:

- Contributing ideas — including ideas you are not sure about
- Asking questions when something is unclear
- Responding when teammates ask for input
- Helping the group make decisions rather than waiting for someone else to decide
- Taking on a task and following through on it

> 💡 **If you tend to be quiet in groups:** commit to one specific role — timekeeper, note-taker, or puzzle lead. A defined role makes contributing much easier than trying to jump into open conversation.
>
> **If you tend to dominate in groups:** commit to explicitly inviting input from two teammates who have not spoken recently. Creating space is a leadership skill.

## STEP 5: Evaluate Your Teammates

After the session, complete a **peer evaluation for each of your team members**.

Rate and briefly comment on each teammate across these dimensions:

| Criterion | What You're Assessing |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Communication** | Did they express ideas clearly and keep the team informed? |
| **Participation** | Did they show up, stay engaged, and contribute consistently? |
| **Collaboration** | Did they work *with* the team rather than around it? |
| **Problem-Solving** | Did they contribute useful thinking toward the puzzles? |
| **Willingness to Listen** | Did they genuinely consider others' ideas? |
| **Negotiation & Compromise** | Did they handle disagreement constructively? |
| **Overall Contribution** | What was their net impact on the team's success? |

### Evaluation Guidelines

- **Be honest.** Inflated evaluations where everyone is rated perfect are not useful feedback and do not reflect real experience.
- **Be professional.** Comment on behavior and contribution, not on personality. *"Joined 45 minutes late and missed the setup discussion"* is useful. *"Didn't seem to care"* is not.
- **Be specific.** One concrete example is worth more than three general adjectives.
- **Be fair.** Base your evaluation on what actually happened during the activity, not on prior impressions or friendships.

## STEP 6: Write Your Individual Reflection

**Length:** 200-300 words

Address each of the following:

1. **What worked well for your team?**
   What specific behaviors, decisions, or dynamics helped you make progress?

2. **What communication challenges did your team face?**
   Be concrete. Where did information get lost, misunderstood, or never shared at all?

3. **How did your team handle disagreement?**
   Describe an actual moment of conflicting opinions. What happened? Was it resolved well or poorly?

4. **What did you learn about your own teamwork and communication style?**
   This is the most important question. Were you a driver, a listener, a coordinator, a quiet contributor? Did that surprise you?

5. **What would you do differently in your next team project?**
   Name one specific, concrete change — not a general intention like "communicate better."

> 💡 **Write honestly.** Reflections that claim everything went perfectly are the least credible and the least valuable. The most useful reflection names something that went wrong and what you personally would change about it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if my team cannot agree on a time?**
Propose a specific date and time yourself, then ask for confirmations. If a genuine scheduling deadlock persists after good-faith effort, contact your instructor **before** the deadline — not after. Document your attempts to coordinate.

**What if some teammates never respond?**
Complete the activity with the members who do participate, and document who was unreachable. Your grade reflects your own participation and contribution, not your teammates' absence.

**What if we do not escape in time?**
You can still earn full credit. The grade is based on your participation, evaluation quality, and reflection depth — not on whether the puzzles were solved.

**Can we split the session across two days?**
It is discouraged but permitted if scheduling requires it. Be aware that regaining lost context costs significant time.

**Can I complete the Escape Room by myself?**
No. This is a team assignment, and the learning objectives are entirely about working with other people. Individual completion does not satisfy the requirement.
