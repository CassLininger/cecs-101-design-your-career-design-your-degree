# Career Exploration Map Activity

This activity asks you to research at least three careers in depth using professional tools. You will build a Career Exploration Map — a structured, comparative overview of the careers you find most compelling. This becomes a reference document you will use in Section 4.5 when building your Career Plan.

---

## Step 1 — Select Three Careers to Research

Based on your RIASEC profile, your self-assessment, and your Ideation Intentions, identify three careers you want to explore in depth.

💡 **Guidance on selection:** Choose careers that genuinely interest you — even if you are uncertain about them.
- One should be a career you have considered seriously.
- One can be a career that surprised you in your RIASEC results.
- One can be a career that feels like a stretch but has always intrigued you.

**Career 2:** [Cybersecurity Manager / Computer and Information Systems Manager]
**Career 3:** [Penetration Tester]

## Career 3: [Penetration Tester]

**Source(s) used:**
- [ ] O*NET
- [ ] BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook
- [ ] LinkedIn
- [ ] Other

**What people in this career actually do day-to-day:**
*(Summarize the core tasks and responsibilities — in your own words.)*
[Penetration Testers perform authorized security tests that simulate how an attacker might try to compromise a system. Their job is to identify vulnerabilities before a real attacker can take advantage of them. Their work can include researching vulnerabilities, testing networks or applications in approved environments, documenting what they find, and explaining how organizations can improve their security. They also have to constantly learn about new threats and testing techniques.]

**Work environment:**
*(Where do they work? Office? Field? Hospital? Remote? With people? Alone? Physical? Sedentary?)*
[Most of the work is computer-based and takes place indoors in an office or remote setting. Penetration Testers can work individually while performing testing, but they also have to communicate with security teams, IT employees, managers, and clients. O*NET indicates that sitting and computer work make up a large part of the job and that accuracy is extremely important.]

**Required education and training:**
*(What degree level is typically required? Any certifications, licenses, or graduate degrees needed?)*
[O*NET places Penetration Testers in Job Zone Four, meaning considerable preparation is normally required. A bachelor's degree is common, although there are other routes into the field. Previous cybersecurity or IT experience and hands-on training can be especially important. Certifications focused on ethical hacking and penetration testing can also help demonstrate technical skills.]
