# Career Vision Statement Activity

This activity guides you through a structured writing process to develop a meaningful Career Vision Statement. You will build it in layers — starting with raw material from your previous work, developing a draft, refining it, and then committing to a final version.

---

## Step 1 — Gather Your Raw Material

Before writing, pull together the following from your previous work:

- Your Personal Empathy Map insights
- Your SWOT Analysis
- Your Personal POV Statement
- Your RIASEC Profile
- Your Career Exploration Map

---

## Step 2 — Answer the Foundation Questions

**What kind of work do I want to do?**
*(Think about tasks, not job titles. What activities do you want to fill your professional days with? What problems do you want to work on?)*
[I want to work on problems involving computers, networks, and cybersecurity. I enjoy figuring out why something is happening, finding weaknesses or problems, and determining how they can be fixed or prevented. I would like my work to involve investigating security issues, working with security tools, and protecting systems from threats.]

**Who do I want to serve or impact?**
*(Who are the people, communities, or sectors your work will benefit? Be as specific as you can.)*
[I want my work to help organizations and the people who depend on their technology. Whether I eventually work for a private company, government organization, or another type of organization, I want what I do to help keep important systems and information secure.]

**What values do I want my career to embody?**
[I want my career to represent thing like responsibility, reliability, curiosity, honesty, and constant improvement. Cybersecurity changes constantly, so I also want to be someone who is willing to keep learning instead of becoming comfortable with only what I already know.]

**What kind of professional do I want to become?**
*(What qualities, character traits, or reputation do you want to be known for in your field?)*
[I want to become someone people can depend on when there is a difficult problem that needs to be solved. I want to be confident in what I know while also being willing to admit when I do not know something and take the time to learn it. Eventually, I want to have enough experience and knowledge that others trust my judgment and abilities.]

**What does success look like for me — not financially, but meaningfully?**
*(Set salary aside for a moment. What would make you feel that your career was truly worthwhile?)*
[Success to me would mean reaching a point where I know that the work I am doing actually matters. I would like to be able to look back and see that I continued improving instead of wasting the abilities I know I have. I would also want to know that my work helped protect people or organizations and that I became someone others could rely on.]

---

## Step 3 — Draft Your Career Vision Statement

Using your foundation questions as raw material, write a first draft of your Career Vision Statement. Aim for 3–5 sentences. Use the structure below as a guide — but feel free to depart from it once you have a draft:

> "My career vision is to [] for/with [who you serve], so that [impact or outcome you want to create]. I want to be known as a professional who [qualities/values]. I see myself building a career in [field or sector] where I can [specific contribution], grounded in my commitment to [core value]."

**My Draft Career Vision Statement:**
[My professional aspiration is to establish a career in cybersecurity where I can analyze problems, identify security vulnerabilities, and help protect the systems and information that individuals and businesses depend on. I hope to become a reliable, knowledgeable, and inquisitive professional who is always willing to learn as technology continues to advance. I want to apply my technical knowledge, interest in technology, and problem-solving skills to make a positive impact while continuing to challenge myself and grow throughout my career.]

---

## Step 4 — Refine and Finalize

Read your draft aloud (or read it slowly in your head). Ask yourself:

- Does this sound like me, or does it sound like what I think I should say?
- Is it specific enough to be meaningful?
- Is it broad enough to allow for growth?
- Does it excite me when I read it?

Revise as needed and write your final version below.

**My Final Career Vision Statement:**

> [My professional goal is a position in cybersecurity where I can take on a problem, analyze an adversary, and defend the technology and data my clients and companies rely on. I aim to be the employee that is reliable and experienced but never complacent and continues to broaden their own knowledge base. I want to advance to roles with increasing responsibility and be the person that others can turn to when they face that large security challenge. I want to be able to make a difference in the workplace and take pride in my career.]

---

💡 **Note:** Your Career Vision Statement is a living document. You are not locked into it forever. You will revisit and revise it in Module 5 and beyond as your self-knowledge deepens and your career exploration continues.
